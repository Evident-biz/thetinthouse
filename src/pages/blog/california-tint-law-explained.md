California's tint rules confuse a lot of people, and some shops will install whatever you ask for. Here's what you actually need to know.

## Front side windows: 70%

Your driver and front passenger windows must let in **at least 70% of light**. That number includes the factory glass, which already blocks some light on its own. So in practice, the front windows can only take a very light or clear film.

The good news: modern ceramic films can reject a lot of heat even when they're nearly clear, so you can still get a cooler cabin up front and stay legal.

## Rear side windows and back glass: your call

Behind the driver, you can go as dark as you like, as long as your vehicle has **outside mirrors on both sides**. That's why you'll see a lot of cars with legal 70% up front and 20% or 35% in the back.

## Windshield: top 4 inches

You can put a non-reflective tint strip on the **top 4 inches** of the windshield. A sun strip makes a big difference with morning and evening glare on the 5, 99 and 580.

## Other rules

- No mirror-like tint that's more reflective than standard glass.
- No red, amber or blue tint on the windshield or front side windows.

## What if your tint is too dark?

You can get a fix-it ticket, which means paying to remove the film, getting the correction signed off, and possibly a fine. That costs a lot more than doing it right the first time.
